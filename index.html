Introducing Kubernaut

Your AI-Powered OpenShift Operations Copilot

Kubernaut is an intelligent remediation platform that investigates like your best SRE and executes like your most careful operator.

Investigate
LLM-powered root cause analysis. Queries pods, logs, metrics, events -- exactly what an SRE would do manually. 		Remediate
Selects proven workflows from a curated catalog. Executes via Tekton pipelines with safety validation at every step. 		Verify
Confirms the fix actually worked. Checks health, metrics, and alert resolution before closing the loop.
This is NOT a black box. Every investigation step is auditable. Every remediation requires approval for critical workloads. Humans remain in control -- Kubernaut just eliminates the toil.

SPEAKER NOTES -- Slide 2 (6:00 - 12:00)

Opening line: "So what is Kubernaut? In one sentence: it's an AI platform that does the investigation work your SREs do at 3am -- but it does it in seconds, consistently, and with a full audit trail."

The three pillars -- walk through each:

  • Investigate: "When an alert fires, Kubernaut's AI agent -- powered by large language models -- does exactly what your best engineer would do. It reads pod logs, checks Prometheus metrics, looks at recent deployments, examines events. But it does this in 10-15 seconds instead of 20 minutes."
  • Remediate: "It doesn't freestyle. It selects from a pre-approved workflow catalog -- think of it like a curated library of runbooks that are actually executable. Scale a deployment, restart a pod, roll back a release -- all proven, all tested."
  • Verify: "This is where most automation falls short. Kubernaut doesn't just 'fire and forget.' It actively checks: did the alert resolve? Are pods healthy? Are latency metrics back to normal? If not, it escalates to a human."

Address the "black box" concern proactively: "I know what some of you are thinking -- 'AI making changes to production? That sounds terrifying.' And you're right to be cautious. That's why I want to be very clear: Kubernaut has mandatory approval gates. For any action above a confidence threshold, a human must approve. Every step is logged. Every action has an audit trail. This isn't autonomous chaos -- it's supervised automation."

Positioning vs. existing tools: "This doesn't replace Prometheus, Grafana, or PagerDuty. It sits downstream from them. Your monitoring detects the problem; Kubernaut investigates and fixes it. Think of it as the missing link between 'alert fired' and 'problem resolved.'"

Anticipated question -- "How is this different from PagerDuty automation or Rundeck?" Answer: "Those tools execute pre-defined scripts. Kubernaut actually investigates first -- it determines WHAT to do based on the specific context, then selects the right workflow. It's the difference between a button that always restarts a pod vs. an engineer who figures out whether it needs a restart, a scale-up, or a rollback."

SPEAKER NOTES -- Slide 3 (12:00 - 18:00)

Opening line: "Let me walk you through exactly what happens when an alert fires in our OpenShift cluster."

Walk through the flow with a real scenario:

  • "Let's say Prometheus fires a CrashLoopBackOff alert for the payment-api pod in production."
  • Gateway: "The alert webhook hits Kubernaut's Gateway. It deduplicates -- if we already have an active remediation for this exact issue, it doesn't create a second one. It normalizes the alert into a standard format regardless of whether it came from Prometheus, Grafana, or a K8s event."
  • Signal Processing: "The signal gets enriched -- Kubernaut pulls in the namespace labels, the deployment spec, the owner chain (this pod belongs to this ReplicaSet which belongs to this Deployment). It classifies the environment as production, assigns priority P0."
  • AI Analysis: "Here's where the magic happens. The AI agent -- think of it as a virtual SRE -- starts investigating. It reads the pod logs, checks recent deployments, queries Prometheus for memory trends. In 10-15 seconds it identifies: 'OOM kill due to memory leak introduced in last deployment.' It selects the workflow: 'rollback to previous revision.'"
  • Execution: "Because this is production and confidence is 85%, it creates a remediation request. Since our policy requires approval above a threshold, a Slack notification goes to the on-call. They approve with one click. Tekton executes the rollback."
  • Verification: "After execution, Kubernaut waits and checks: are the pods healthy? Is the OOM alert resolved? Are latency metrics back to normal? Only when all checks pass does it mark the remediation as successful."

OpenShift-specific points to emphasize:

  • "This is completely Kubernetes-native. Everything is a Custom Resource. You can watch the remediation progress with 'oc get rr' -- just like any other OpenShift resource."
  • "It respects OpenShift security -- TLS profiles, SCCs, RBAC. It doesn't bypass your security model; it works within it."
  • "Deployment is a single Helm chart. It runs in its own namespace and only operates on namespaces you explicitly opt in."

Anticipated question -- "What about our air-gapped clusters?" Answer: "Kubernaut supports air-gap deployment. The workflow catalog uses OCI bundles that can be mirrored to an internal registry. The LLM can be a locally hosted model like Ollama -- no external API calls required."

Anticipated question -- "Does it need cluster-admin?" Answer: "No. It uses dedicated service accounts with minimum required RBAC. Each service has its own scoped permissions. You can audit exactly what it has access to."

Safety, Compliance, and Control

Built for Regulated Environments

Human-in-the-Loop Approval

Medium-confidence actions require explicit operator approval via Slack or console. High-confidence, low-risk actions (like pod restarts) can auto-execute per policy.

SOC2 / FedRAMP Audit Trail

Every action produces an immutable, hash-chained audit record. Full remediation lifecycle reconstructable from audit events alone. 7-year retention.

Scope Management

Only acts on namespaces labeled kubernaut.ai/managed=true. Everything else is invisible. Deny-by-default.

Effectiveness Verification

Post-remediation health checks confirm the fix worked. If metrics don't improve, escalates to human review. Never marks "done" prematurely.

Consecutive failure protection: After 3 failed attempts, Kubernaut blocks further automation and escalates to manual review.

SPEAKER NOTES -- Slide 4 (18:00 - 24:00)

Opening line: "I know the first question in everyone's mind is: 'This sounds great, but how do we make sure it doesn't break production?' Let me address that head-on."

This is the slide that builds trust with leadership. Walk through each quadrant:

  • Approval gates: "Think of it like a pull request for infrastructure changes. The AI proposes, a human approves. For routine, high-confidence actions -- like restarting a crashing pod that's crashed 5 times before -- you can configure auto-approval. But anything novel, anything in a critical namespace, requires human sign-off. You define the policy."
  • Audit trail: "This is where it gets important for compliance. Every single action Kubernaut takes produces an immutable audit event. These events are hash-chained -- meaning you can't tamper with them without breaking the chain. We can reconstruct the entire remediation lifecycle from audit alone -- who approved what, when, why the AI chose that action, what the outcome was. This maps directly to SOC2 CC8.1 and FedRAMP AU-2 controls."
  • Scope management: "Kubernaut doesn't have free rein over your cluster. You explicitly opt in namespaces by adding a label. No label, no access. Period. This is deny-by-default -- exactly how you'd expect a security-conscious tool to behave."
  • Verification: "The most common failure in automation is 'fire and forget.' Kubernaut verifies. After every remediation, it checks: did the alert resolve? Are health checks passing? Are metrics back to baseline? If not, it doesn't pretend the problem is fixed -- it escalates."

The consecutive failure point: "And there's a built-in circuit breaker. If the same issue fails remediation three times in a row, Kubernaut stops trying and pages a human. It won't thrash your cluster with repeated failed attempts."

Anticipated question -- "What about our compliance requirements?" Answer: "Kubernaut was designed from day one for regulated environments. The audit trail meets SOC2 Type II and FedRAMP control requirements. Every event has actor attribution, timestamps, correlation IDs, and outcome tracking. Your auditors can query the system directly."

Anticipated question -- "Can we limit what actions it can take?" Answer: "Absolutely. The workflow catalog is curated. You decide which workflows are available. You can restrict by namespace, by action type, by confidence threshold. The system is as conservative or as automated as you configure it to be."
